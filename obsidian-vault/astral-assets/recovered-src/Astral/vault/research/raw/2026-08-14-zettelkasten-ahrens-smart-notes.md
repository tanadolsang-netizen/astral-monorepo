## The five note categories

1. **Fleeting notes** — quick, temporary capture (handwritten or otherwise) made in the moment an idea occurs, while doing something else. Meant to be processed and discarded within days — not meant to survive long-term.
2. **Literature notes** — brief, in-your-own-words summaries of what you read, tied to a source reference (citation-manager style).
3. **Permanent notes** — self-contained, durable, "written as if for print" — the ideas worth keeping, rewritten so they stand alone without needing the original source's context.
4. **Zettels** — the actual interconnected slip-box entries; the permanent notes once they're linked into the web of existing notes.
5. **Project notes** — notes scoped to a specific piece of writing/output, living outside the permanent system.

## The core mechanism

The staged pipeline (fleeting → literature → permanent → linked → project output) forces re-engagement with material at each stage — rewriting an idea in your own words is what drives internalization, not passive collection/highlighting. The linked slip-box becomes an active thinking partner: relationships and gaps between ideas surface because notes are forced to connect to existing ones rather than sit in an unconnected pile.
