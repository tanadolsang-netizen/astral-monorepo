## What the article covers

Why Jung — in the last thirty years of his life — turned to medieval/Renaissance alchemy as a core resource for depth psychology, framed as an introduction to a Murray Stein lecture series on the topic.

## Key points

- Jung found that alchemical imagery and concepts uniquely matched what he was already seeing in patients' dreams and in the unconscious's own symbolic productions — alchemy functioned as external confirmation that the psyche's transformative process is purposeful, not random.
- Alchemy provided Jung a **metaphorical vocabulary**: the alchemists' literal effort to transmute base matter into gold became, for Jung, a projection screen for an unconscious psychological process — turning unrefined, unconscious material into integrated, conscious wholeness.
- Directly tied to **individuation** — alchemical symbolism illuminates the journey toward psychological wholeness (becoming one's authentic, integrated self), and Jung's Collected Works volumes on alchemy sit alongside his volumes on transference and analytical psychology, i.e. alchemy wasn't a side interest but central to his mature theory.
- Article references Jung's own line: "True alchemy was never a business or a career, but a genuine opus to be achieved by quiet, self-sacrificing work" — underscoring that alchemy-as-metaphor is about slow, unglamorous inner labor, not a dramatic epiphany.
