## What the source is

Waite's own companion book to the deck he co-created — the **original, primary-source explanation of what the standard Rider-Waite-Smith card meanings are and where they came from**. Every modern RWS-tradition card-meaning site (Biddy Tarot, Labyrinthos, etc.) ultimately traces its divinatory meanings back to this book, whether or not it cites Waite directly.

## Book content

- A guide to the symbolism and divinatory meaning of all 78 cards, paired with Pamela Colman Smith's illustrations.
- Historical tracing of the tarot's earlier symbolic traditions and Waite's critique of prior (pre-RWS) theories about the cards' origins and meanings.
- Practical divination method, including the **Celtic Cross spread** — which Waite's book is largely responsible for popularizing as a standard layout.
- Notable structural choices that became the RWS standard: Strength placed as card VIII and Justice as card XI (reversed from some earlier decks' numbering), and — significantly — fully illustrated **pip/minor arcana cards** (Six of Pentacles, Two of Cups, Seven of Swords, Eight of Wands, etc. all get complete narrative scenes rather than just repeated suit symbols), which is what let RWS-tradition readers assign specific divinatory meanings to minor arcana cards at all instead of relying purely on numerology + suit.
