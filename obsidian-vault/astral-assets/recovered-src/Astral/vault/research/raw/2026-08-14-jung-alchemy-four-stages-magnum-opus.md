## What the article covers

A stage-by-stage breakdown of Jung's alchemical model of psychological transformation — the **magnum opus** ("Great Work") mapped onto four classical alchemical phases.

## Core concepts

- **Prima materia** — the raw, unrefined starting material of the psyche (the "lead"): everything about the self that is unconscious, undifferentiated, or unintegrated at the start of the work.
- **Magnum Opus** — the complete process of transmutation toward psychological wholeness; not a single event but a sustained "work."
- **Philosopher's Stone** — the alchemical end-goal, standing in for the fully integrated Self reached through individuation.

## The four stages

1. **Nigredo (Blackening)** — the process begins with shadow confrontation. Unconscious material surfaces as chaos and confusion, breaking down the ego's prior, idealized self-image. Quoted Jung: the unconscious is "the fly in the ointment" that ruins naive self-satisfaction.
2. **Albedo (Whitening)** — purification and clarity follow; opposing inner tensions start to integrate and self-knowledge deepens. This is where Jung locates anima/animus integration — which he called the relationship's "masterpiece," contrasted with shadow work's "apprentice-piece."
3. **Citrinitas (Yellowing)** — a stage of spiritual awakening/illumination as higher consciousness emerges through synthesis, often marked (in Jung's own life, per the article) by significant dreams or an inner-guide figure — Jung's own "Philemon" is cited as an example.
4. **Rubedo (Reddening)** — completion: conscious and unconscious unite into a resilient, authentic, individuated personality.
