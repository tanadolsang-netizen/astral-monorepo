## What the article covers

A scholarly (though confessionally evangelical) walkthrough of the two competing interpretations of the 144,000 named in Revelation 7 and 14, arguing for the symbolic reading over the literal one.

## The number's symbolism

Apocalyptic literature uses numbers symbolically, not as head-counts. 144,000 = 12 (tribes of Israel) squared, then multiplied by 1,000 — "a symbolic way of designating the entire people of God," not a literal census.

## Two scholarly camps

- **Literal view:** the 144,000 are actual Jewish believers; dispensationalist readers place them specifically within a future seven-year tribulation period, sealed from among the twelve tribes.
- **Symbolic view (the article's position, following Schreiner):** the 144,000 represent all Christians throughout history — Jews and Gentiles alike — who remain faithful under persecution. The "sealing" is protection/authentication as God's people, not a literal subgroup.

## Six reasons offered for the symbolic reading

1. Apocalyptic numbers are inherently symbolic, not literal.
2. Revelation 7:9 has John *hear* "144,000" and then *see* an "uncountable multitude" — the same group described two different ways (a number vs. an image of totality).
3. "Israel" in Revelation's usage includes all believers, not narrowly ethnic Jews.
4. No mechanism exists to verify literal tribal genealogies for a sealed group.
5. Revelation 14 describes the group as redeemed "from humanity" broadly, not exclusively from Israel.
6. The tribe list itself is irregular — Dan is omitted (possibly linked to idolatry associations elsewhere in scripture) and Joseph/Manasseh appear in place of expected tribes — internal evidence the list isn't meant as literal genealogy but as a stylized "twelve."

## Theological upshot

Those sealed represent "the true and new Israel" — protected and set apart not by literal bloodline but by faithfulness. The article frames the correct response to this passage as gratitude for inclusion through grace, not anxiety about qualifying for a literal headcount.
