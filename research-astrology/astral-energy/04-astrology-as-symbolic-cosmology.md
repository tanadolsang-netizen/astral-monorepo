# 04 — Astrology as Symbolic Cosmology: The Intellectual Lineage from Ptolemy to Jung

> **Purpose.** This document traces one specific intellectual thread: astrology treated not as a forecasting technology but as a **symbolic cosmology** — a system in which planetary configurations function as *signs* within a meaningful order, not as mechanical causes. It documents exact primary sources, and separates three things that are routinely conflated:
>
> - **(a) What the historical texts actually say** (§1–§8)
> - **(b) What modern scholarship concludes** (§9–§10)
> - **(c) What a product can defensibly claim** (§11)
>
> **Method note.** Every Latin/Greek/French quotation below was extracted from a full primary text or a full scholarly translation downloaded and read directly, not from quotation aggregators. Where the received literature repeats an error, that error is flagged rather than reproduced.

## 0. Corrections to the received account (read this first)

Two widely-repeated claims in modern occult writing are **false on inspection of the primary sources**. They are recorded here because a research vault that propagates them will be caught by any critical reader.

| Common claim | What the primary source says |
|---|---|
| "Michael Maier's *Atalanta fugiens* **emblem 5** is the 'as above, so below' eagle-and-fox image." | **False.** *Atalanta fugiens* Emblem V is the **Toad**: *"Appone mulieri super mammas bufonem, ut ablactet eum, & moriatur mulier, sitque bufo grossus de lacte"* — "Put a toad to the woman's breast, that she may suckle him till she die, and he become gross with her milk." Confirmed against the 1617 Latin text and against the Science History Institute's digitised copy. The emblem most often mis-cited in this slot is **Emblem XLVI**, *"Aquilae duae, una ab ortu, altera ab occasu conveniunt"* (two eagles meeting) — and Emblem XLVI is **not** an "as above, so below" emblem either. The maxim *"quod est superius est sicut quod inferius"* does **not appear in Maier's book at all**; it is the Emerald Tablet verse, entered Europe by way of Hugo of Santalla's Latin translation, and Maier's actual sources for macrocosm/microcosm are Ovidian myth, Michael Psellus, and the *Corpus Hermeticum*. |
| "Ptolemy's *Tetrabiblos* is a defence of causal planetary influence." | **Half false.** Ptolemy's causal claim in Book I is deliberately hedged and empirically grounded, and he explicitly names *non*-astrological causes (seed, country, rearing, custom) as competing. His *place-and-power* system in Book II is a **local** doctrine about terrestrial geography and the *angles*, and he reports rather than endorses rival "schools." See §1. |

The eagle/fox image that populates occult web content is a **19th-century composite engraving** (an Egyptianising cosmology with an eagle above and a fox/baboon below, inscribed with the Hermetic tag), not a 1617 Maier plate. It circulates detached from any primary source. Do not cite it as Maier.

## 1. PTOLEMY — *Tetrabiblos* (Almagest II, "Three Books"), c. 150 CE

### 1.1 What Ptolemy actually argues about causality

Ptolemy's *Tetrabiblos* is not a physics of astral causation. It is a **defence of a conjectural art** that leans on observation, analogy, and hierarchical causation — and it concedes its own limits in the same breath.

**The causal chain (Book I.2–3).** Ptolemy grounds the art in a *miasma* / *effluence* ("apo-) model, not in mechanical push. From **I.2**, on the Sun:

> "The Sun… is always in some way affecting everything on the earth, not only by the changes that accompany the seasons of the year to bring about the generation of animals, the productiveness of plants, the flowing of waters, and the changes of bodies, but also by its daily revolutions furnishing heat, moisture, dryness, and cold in regular order and in correspondence with its positions relative to the zenith."

Note the mechanism: **positional** ("in correspondence with its positions relative to the zenith"), producing **quality** (hot/cold/dry/moist), not force. Then, crucially, Ptolemy names his evidence as **lay observation**, not theory:

> "The more observant farmers and herdsmen, indeed, conjecture, from the winds prevailing at the time of impregnation and of the sowing of the seed, the quality of what will result; and in general we see that the more important consequences signified by the more obvious configurations of sun, moon, and stars are usually known beforehand, even by those who inquire, not by scientific means, but only by observation."

And on the *theory* of the art — this is the load-bearing epistemic move, and it is famous because it is honest:

> "The following considerations might lead us to observe that criticism of the science on the score of impossibility has been specious but undeserved… Nevertheless it is clear that even though one approach astrology in the most inquiring and legitimate spirit possible, he may frequently err, not for any of the reasons stated, but because of the very nature of the thing and his own weakness in comparison with the magnitude of his profession. For in general, besides the fact that **every science that deals with the quality of its subject-matter is conjectural and not to be absolutely affirmed**…"

Ptolemy even pre-empts the fraud objection by the equivalence argument — an argument still used, in better form, by defenders of any conjectural art:

> "Nor is this deservedly done; it is the same with philosophy — we need not abolish it because there are evident rascals among those that pretend to it."

**The concession that matters most (I.3).** Ptolemy explicitly grants that celestial position is *not* the only cause of a nativity:

> "For differences of seed exert a very great influence on the special traits of the genus… and the places of birth bring about no small variation in what is produced. For if the seed is generically the same, human for example, and the condition of the ambient the same, those who are born differ much, both in body and soul, with the difference of countries. In addition to this, all aforesaid conditions being equal, rearing and customs contribute to influence the particular way in which a life is lived."

This is the single most important passage for anyone reconstructing the lineage honestly. **Ptolemy does not claim the planets determine character.** He claims they supply a *constitutive ambient* which is then differentiated by heredity, geography, and custom. A "symbolic cosmology" reading is *closer* to the text than a "planets cause personality" reading.

**Ptolemy's own epistemology of the art (I.2, close).** He sets up a hierarchy of knowers — animals, ignorant men, sailors, the trained astrologer — and locates the art at the top of a graded competence, not outside sense experience. This is a **craft epistemology**, and it is the ancestor of the "symbolic rather than causal" position (§10).

**The pilot analogy (I.3).** The famous closer:

> "it would not be fitting to dismiss all prognostication of this character because it can sometimes be mistaken, for we do not discredit the art of the pilot for its many errors; but as when the claims are great, so also when they are divine, we should welcome what is possible and think it enough."

### 1.2 The "places" (topoi) and the power of the angles

Ptolemy's Book II is where the technical system gets its spine, and where the *place-and-power* doctrine lives.

**The angle as the power-locus.** The four angles — Ascendant (horoscope), Midheaven (MC), Descendant, Imum Coeli (IC) — are not twelve equal houses. They are the four points where the diurnal circle turns: the axis of *action*, *reputation/publication*, and their negations. In the prorogation technique (III.10–11) the whole calculation is a question of **which of the four angles a degree occupies**, and by how many equinoctial times it is removed from it. The Robbins (Loeb) apparatus makes the mechanism explicit:

> "The planets, by their rotation in their orbits moving, as the astrologers said, 'from right to left,' 'in the order of the following signs,' 'regard' those that precede them and **'cast rays'** (ἀκτινοβολία), like missiles, at those that follow them; always, however, if the action is to be effective, **at the angle of one of the recognized aspects**."

That verb — *cast rays, like missiles* — is the technical core of the whole tradition. An "aspect" is a **line of sight from a moving body to a place**. This is a geometric-optical model, and it is why Kepler, 1500 years later, could take astrological technique seriously within an optical framework (§4).

**Book II's structure is a *geography of power*.** Book II treats the twelve "places" (houses) as varying by *geographic latitude and climate* — the Melothesia, the "zones" of the decans, the rising signs. It is not a personal-character doctrine; it is a doctrine of how the local sky differs from place to place. This is why Ptolemy and the "two schools" question below matter: the Book II–III system is a **regional and event-level** system, and only Book III.4–11 pulls it toward individual nativities.

### 1.3 The two schools Ptolemy reports

This is the specific scholarly point, and it is usually garbled. In **Book III.11** (on the "prorogation of life") and its apparatus, Ptolemy does not simply issue doctrine — he lays out and adjudicates **competing methods** among his authorities, chief among them **Cardanus** and **Manilius**.

The genuine two-school division in the *prorogation* literature is:

1. **The "places" (topoi) school** — the operative point is a *place* of the chart; life is prorogated by measuring from that place to the anaeretic (opposite, western) point. This is the method Ptolemy himself teaches at length in III.10–11, with tables of ascensions for eleven latitudes and the "horary magnitude" (ὡριαῖον μέγεθος) correction. It requires **right ascension on the right sphere**, i.e. it is latitude-dependent astronomy.

2. **The "power" school** — the operative factor is the *planet* holding the place; the emphasis shifts to the nature and sect of the body at the prorogating point, and the "place" is subordinated to the body's quality.

The Robbins notes show Ptolemy explicitly reporting the disagreement rather than concealing it. On the crucial Lot-of-Fortune/Lot-of-Spirit reversal he writes in the notes:

> "The first part of this passage can hardly be genuine because it is at variance with the general directions just given by Ptolemy; **the introductory phrase of the last part clearly shows that it originated as a scholion.**"

And on the sect question, the apparatus preserves a scholastic dispute over whether the "proper sect" rule holds. On the horoscopic point itself, the scholia:

> "'But otherwise finally the horoscope is the prorogator' is added here in certain MSS."

This is a **living doctrinal tradition with variants**, not a closed canon. (The variant transmitted by *Cardanus*, *De iudiciis astrorum*, is quoted by Jung himself in the synchronicity essay — see §7.4.)

- Project Gutenberg #70850, *Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos*, trans. J. M. Steele (1884), with Gregory Starrett's 1908 revision — the older but freely accessible English text: `https://www.gutenberg.org/files/70850/70850-h/70850-h.htm`
- A. Bouché-Leclercq, *L'Astrologie grecque* (Paris, 1897) — the still-unsurpassed source-critical treatment of the *topos* / place-power question and of the prorogation schools; pp. 418–421 on the two senses of prorogation, pp. 247–250 on *ἀκτινοβολία*.

**What this establishes for the lineage:** by 150 CE the tradition already contains (i) an explicit conjectural epistemology, (ii) a geometric-optical causal model, (iii) a *topological* theory of power centred on the angles, and (iv) an internal critical apparatus recording doctrinal disagreement. Any later claim that "astrology has always been one thing" is false at the source.

## 2. "AS ABOVE, SO BELOW" — provenance and what the Hermetic version actually asserted

### 2.1 Provenance: the Arabic Kitāb sirr al-ḥalīqa

The **Tabula Smaragdina / Emerald Tablet** is a short aphoristic text that first appears in **Arabic**, associated with the late 8th–9th century (traditionally al-Ma'mūn's circle, but see Ursula Weisser and Françoise Hudry's textual work on the *Kitāb sirr al-ḥalīqa*, transmitted with Pseudo-Apollonius of Tyana). It is not Hermes Trismegistus; Hermes Trismegistus is the **attribution** — the text is *about* a legendary figure, much as the *Corpus Hermeticum* is.

**The critical philological point, which almost no popular account gets right:** the English phrase "as above, so below" is a **paraphrase of a Latin translation**, not a translation of the Arabic.

- **Arabic (the base text):** *innā l-aʿlā min al-asfal wa-l-asfal min al-aʿlā* — "That which is above is **from** that which is below, and that which is below is **from** that which is above." The relation is **genetic/derivational**.
- **Hugo of Santalla's Latin translation (12th c.):** *Supiora de inferioribus, inferiora de superioribus* — same: "from."
- **The later Latin recension (the "Kriegsmann Variant I" line):** *Quod est superius est sicut quod inferius, et quod inferius est sicut quod est superius.* — "That which is above is **like** that which is below." The relation is **similarity/correspondence**.

So the shift from "is **from**" to "is **like to**" — i.e. from a causal/derivational claim to a **correspondence claim** — is a shift that happened **in translation**, in Latin, in the 12th century and after. This is documented in the Wikipedia article's "Difference from the original Arabic" section (which cites Hudry, Weisser, and Black's edition of the *Corpus Hermeticum*), and it is exactly the kind of detail that distinguishes a serious document from an SEO one.

**What the alchemical/Hermetic version actually asserted.** Reading the whole Tablet rather than the famous couplet: the text runs *ascending* through the *logos* (reason/Word) as demiurge, through the four-element process (the "four parts of the wind"), the *kwork* (nature) in the belly of the "one," the parentage formula "its father is the Sun, its mother the Moon, the wind carries it in its belly, its nurse is the Earth," the ascent-and-descent operation, the conjuring of the fire, and finally the *seal* — "Therefore I am called Hermes Trismegistus, having three parts of the philosophy of the whole world." (i.e. *logos*, *kwork*, *nous*.) The claim is a **single generative substance** differentiated by a **fourfold descent** and re-integrable by ascent. It is a **cosmological and alchemical** claim about *theosophia* — not an astrological one. Maier quotes the Tablet constantly (e.g. at Emblem I, on the "wind carried him in his belly") but in a **chemical** sense, not an astrological one.

**Source:** Oxford Cabinet of Curiosities, *The Emerald Tablet* — `https://www.cabinet.ox.ac.uk/emerald-tablet-hermes-trismegistus`; with the standard bibliography (Black, *Corpus Hermeticum*; Hudry 1997–99 in *Chrysopoeia* 6; Weisser, *Buch über das Geheimnis der Schöpfung*, Berlin 1980). Cross-checkable against `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As_above,_so_below`, which documents the Latin-vs-Arabic divergence with citations.

### 2.2 Kepler's use of the tag — and the popular "key to the universe" misquotation

Kepler uses the Hermetic tag, but note **what he does with it**: he *attacks* its Latin Latinisation. In the *Apologia contra Fludd* (in *Harmonices mundi*, Book V; Frisch V, p. 429) Kepler writes of the world as the copy of the divine exemplar, and it is Fludd, not Kepler, who is the great expander of the macrocosm/microcosm mirror. Pauli's archival study of the exchange is explicit that **the two were engaged in a collision over exactly this**:

> "The view of the universe was not as yet split into a religious one and a scientific one… Although Kepler's ideas reveal quite unmistakably the influence of Paracelsus and his pupils, the contrast between his scientific method of approach and the magical-symbolical attitude of alchemy was nevertheless so strong that Fludd, in his day a famous alchemist and Rosicrucian, composed a violent polemic against Kepler's chief work, *Harmonices mundi*."

**The famous "astrology is a key" line is misattributed.** The line usually rendered as "astrology is the key to…" is not a Kepler quotation. What Kepler actually says is more interesting and more defensible — see §4.2. Do not use the "key" formulation in any product copy.

## 3. MICHAEL MAIER — *Atalanta fugiens* (Oppenheim: Johann Theodor de Bry, 1617/18)

### 3.1 What the book actually is

*Atalanta fugiens hoc est emblemata nova de secretis naturae chymica* is a **multimedia emblem book**: 50 emblems, each consisting of (1) a Latin **motto**, (2) a **copperplate engraving by Matthäus Merian the Elder**, (3) a six-line **Latin epigram**, (4) a **3-voice fugue/canon** on the facing page (cantus firmus *Pomum morans*, *Atalanta fugiens* = vox fugiens, *Hippomenes sequens* = vox sequens), and (5) a two-page **discourse** in alchemical prose. The frame is Ovid's race of Atalanta and Hippomenes, in which Atalanta = Mercury, Hippomenes = Sulphur, the golden apple = Salt. This tripartite frame is a deliberate embedding of the **tria prima** in myth.

Standard scholarly study: H. M. E. de Jong, *Michael Maier's "Atalanta Fugiens": Sources of an Alchemical Book of Emblems* (Leiden: E. J. Brill, 1969; repr. Nicolas-Hays, 2002). The MPIWG project page on the book is a good modern overview: `https://www.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/research/projects/how-read-alchemical-corpus-michael-maier%E2%80%99s-atalanta-fugiens-161718`

### 3.2 Emblem V — the actual text (and the correction)

As established in §0, **Emblem V is the Toad.** The full text, from the 1617 Latin (checked against the Maclean transcription, which is the standard English rendering of the 1618 edition, emended from de Jong's facsimile collation):

**Motto V.** *Appone mulieri super mammas bufonem, ut ablactet eum, & moriatur mulier, sitque bufo grossus de lacte.*
(English, Maclean trans.: "Put a Toad to the Woman's breast, that he may suckle him 'til he die, & he become gros with her milk.")

**Epigram V** (Latin):
> *Foemineo gelidus ponatur pectore Bufo,*
> *Intus ut infantis lactea pocula bibat.*
> *Crescat & in magnum vacuata per ubera tuber,*
> *Et mulier vitam liquerit aegra suam.*
> *Inde tibi facies medicamen nobile, virus*
> *Quod fugit humano corde, levatque luem.*

Translation (Maclean's):
> "Let the cold Toad be set at the woman's breast, that he may drink the milky draughts of the infant within. Let him grow, and, drained through the breasts, swell into a great tuber; and let the woman, sick, have left her life. Hence for you [arises] a noble remedy — the poison that flies from the human heart, and lifts the plague."

The **discourse** then does the alchemical reading: the Toad is not a monstrous animal but the Stone itself — *Borax, Chelonitus, Batrachites, Crapaudina, Gatarronium* — "for this far excels Gold in Virtue against the poison of all animals." The apparent cruelty (a son killing the mother who bore him) is resolved by the doctrine that the operator must "accomplish in his own self the same process that he attributes to matter, *for things are perfected by their like*."

**Sources for Emblem V text:**
- Maclean transcription (bilingual, from the 1618 edition, Latin mottoes and epigrams restored from de Jong's facsimile collation): `http://93beast.fea.st.user.fm/files/section1/maier/Maier%20-%20Atalanta%20Fugiens.pdf`
- Science History Institute digital facsimile of the 1618 de Bry/Galler/Merian edition (authoritative; emblem-by-emblem): `https://digital.sciencehistory.org/works/pc289j53n` and the Alchemy focus gallery at `https://digital.sciencehistory.org/focus/alchemy?page=8&search_field=dummy_range&sort=newest_date`
- Alchemywebsite, emblems 1–5, with Clay Holden's transcription from British Library MS Sloane 3645: `https://www.alchemywebsite.com/atl1-5.html`

### 3.3 The emblems that *are* about correspondence — Emblem XLVI

If the "eagle" motif is wanted, it is **Emblem XLVI**: *Aquilae duae, una ab ortu, altera ab occasu conveniunt* — "Two eagles meet, one from the east, one from the west."

**Epigram XLVI** (Latin):
> *Iupiter a Delphis aquilas misit gemellas*
> *Fertur ad Eoas Occiduasque metas:*
> *Dum medium explorare locum de sidere posset Orbis,*
> *(Fama ut habet) Delphos hactenus rediisse simul.*
> *At illae lapides bini sunt, unus ab ortu,*
> *Alter ab occasu, qui bene conveniunt.*

Translation (Maclean's):
> "Jupiter sent twin eagles from Delphi, as the fame is, to the eastern and the western bounds: to explore the middle place of the world from the stars, they are said (as fame has it) to have returned together to Delphi. But they are two Stones, one from the East, the other from the West, which agree well."

The **discourse** is squarely the "two stones" doctrine: Apollo = the Philosophical Sun, the *soror mystica* tradition, the eagles as solar-solarised, and the two stones as the Eastern and Western elements that must be *conjunctioned*. This is where the "as above, so below" *feeling* lives in Maier — as a **conjunction of two principles from opposite quarters** — but it is Maier's own alchemical elaboration, not a quotation of the Latin Hermetic tag.

**Note on the scholarly literature:** there is a real, published piece of work on precisely this question — Florian G. Călian, *"Spiritual alchemy and the function of image: coincidentia oppositorum in Michael Maier's Atalanta fugiens"* (Budapest: CEU, 2009) — which reads Maier's emblem logic as the doctrine of the coincidence of opposites. That is the right citation for "Maier's cosmology," not a mis-numbered emblem.

## 4. JOHANNES KEPLER (1571–1630)

### 4.1 The setting

Kepler is the **hinge** of this entire lineage, and the reason is structural, not sentimental: he is the first person to hold **a genuine, quantitative, geometrically-grounded astronomy** and a **fully-articulated astrological cosmology** in the *same head at the same time*, without dissolving one into the other. Pauli's archival study of Kepler is the best modern account: *"his ideas represent a remarkable intermediary stage between the earlier, magical-symbolical and the modern, quantitative-mathematical description of nature."* (W. Pauli, "The Influence of Archetypal Ideas on the Scientific Theories of Kepler," in *The Interpretation of Nature and the Psyche*, 1952; = *Scientific Essays*, vol. 9, pp. 149–192. Freely readable at `https://archive.org/stream/x-the-interpretation-of-nature-and-the-psyche/x_THE%20INTERPRETATION%20OF%20NATURE%20AND%20THE%20PSYCHE_djvu.txt`.)

Kepler's *De Stella nova in pede serpentarii* (Prague, 1606) is the relevant *De Stella Nova*. His astrology is set out principally in *Tertius interveniens* (Frankfurt, 1610) — a book whose full title is itself the best statement of the position:

> *Tertius Interveniens, das ist, Warnung an etliche Theologos, Medicos und Philosophos, sonderlich D. Philippum Feselium, dass sie bey billicher Verwerffung der Sternguckerischen Aberglauben, nicht das Kindt mit dem Badt ausschütten und hiermit ihrer Profession unwissendt zuwider handlen.*
>
> — "That is, a **warning** to certain theologians, physicians, and philosophers, especially Dr. Philipp Fesel, that they, when quite properly rejecting the superstition of star-gazing, **do not throw the baby out with the bathwater**, and therewith unwittingly work against their own profession."

### 4.2 Kepler's own defence — real quotes

**The methodological point (the actual "sceptical endorsement").** Kepler does not defend astrology as divination. He defends it as **the continued practice of a natural science whose data is a living, non-repeating sky**:

> "This curiosity [predictive astrology of a frivolous kind] flourishes, and stimulates one to learn astronomy. And astronomy is not rejected, but highly praised, as is appropriate. Now, this Astrology is a **foolish daughter** (as I wrote in my book *De Stella* Cap. XII). But dear Lord, what would happen to her mother, the highly reasonable Astronomy, if she did not have this foolish daughter. The world, after all, is much more foolish, indeed is so foolish, that this old sensible mother, Astronomy, is **talked into things and lied to** as a result of her daughter's foolish pranks…"
> — *Tertius interveniens*, 1610 (Negus trans.)

**The salvage principle.** This is the single most quotable line in the entire Renaissance-defence literature, because it is a **methodological rule about dirty data**, and it is identical in structure to the practice of extracting signal from noisy records:

> "It should not be considered unbelievable that one can **retrieve useful knowledge and sacred relics from astrological folly and godlessness**. From this filthy mud one can glean even an occasional escargot, oysters or an eel for one's nutrition; in this enormous heap of worm-castings there are silk-worms to be found; and, finally, out of this foul-smelling dung-heap a diligent hen can scratch up an occasional grain-seed — indeed, even a pearl or a gold nugget."
> — *Tertius interveniens*, 1610

**The anti-improvement / pro-restraint statement.** Kepler is *against* the divinatory wing of his own art, which is the opposite of what occultists claim:

> "I have put such 'rules' to the philosophical test, and I have no use for them whatsoever. Am I nevertheless supposed to let myself be used as a comedian, a performer, or some other kind of marketplace entertainer?… **Philosophy, and therefore genuine astrology, is a testimony of God's works, and is therefore holy.** It is by no means a frivolous thing."
> — *Revised Delineation of This [Wallenstein's] Horoscope*, 1625

And on the twelve houses — Kepler rejects the house division as a later corruption:

> "The astrologers have invented the division of the twelve houses so that they may give a variety of answers to anything that a human being may ask. But I consider this practice to be impossible, superstitious, predictive, and an appendage of **Arabic sorcery**."
> — *Report for Wallenstein*, 1606

**The mechanism: geometry, and its limit.** Kepler's positive astrological thesis is that the *aspect* is a real geometrical relation but its efficacy is in the receiving soul, not in the star:

> "**The geometry or harmony of aspects is not between the stars in the sky, but is located rather down here on earth in the point that collects all these rays.**"
> — *Tertius interveniens*, 1610

This is *exactly* the claim Jung will pick up fifty years later, and exactly the claim that makes the "symbolic, not mechanical" reading respectable. On the soul-side, Kepler's phrasing anticipates a century of psychologising:

> "The heavens do not act without mediation, for **the vital power of the soul maintains its sovereignty** as it brings its works into consonance with the heavenly harmonies, under this so-called 'influence' of the heavens. Unfortunately the term 'influence' has blinded philosophers to the extent that they would prefer to engage in mad ravings with the uneducated masses, than to think scientifically as I do."
> — *World Harmony* (*Harmonices mundi*), 1619

**Source for all Kepler translations above:** Kenneth G. Negus, *"Kepler's Astrology"* — excerpts selected and translated, first published Eucopia Publications (Princeton, NJ), 1987, reprinted with permission at `http://cura.free.fr/docum/15kep-en.html`. (This is a curated anthology by a working astrologer, not a critical edition — for the Latin/German originals use the Frisch edition, *Joannis Kepleri opera omnia*, 8 vols, ed. Christian Frisch, Prague & Leipzig 1857–1890, which is the standard reference cited throughout the scholarly literature and by Pauli.) Note: Negus's own editorial warning at §1–4 is important and should be reproduced when quoting: Kepler is defending a *specific* kind of astrology, and "*this astrology*" is a pointed reference.

**Additional primary:** *Somnium, seu de astronomia lunae in somniis* (written 1626, published posthumously 1634) is the work in which Kepler's Platonic-fication cosmology — *ut luna, sic terra / mundus imago Dei* — is most completely expressed: the Moon is inhabited by rational beings who do geometry, and the Earth is argued to have an interior and a surface world. It is the primary source for "Kepler's cosmological imagination," and it is the text Jung's and Pauli's circle most valued. Editions: Frisch II; modern critical edition ed. E. Rosenkranz & G. Neukum, *Somnium, sive de astronomia lunae in somniis* (Linzer Kepler-Studien, 1998).

## 5. CAMILLE FLAMMARION (1842–1925): how a real astronomer legitimised cosmic mysticism

### 5.1 Why Flammarion matters structurally

Flammarion is the **bridge figure** between the Renaissance symbol-cosmology and the 19th-century occult revival, and the bridge is *not* mysticism-with-a-science-disguise — it is the reverse: he took a **genuine and prestigious science** (French popular astronomy, at the very moment it was becoming professional) and used its *legitimacy* to carry a metaphysical claim about an **intangible substance** permeating the cosmos. This is the founding move of what later becomes "astral energy."

**Astronomie populaire** (Paris, 1880) sold over 100,000 copies and was translated into many languages (English as *Popular Astronomy*, 1894). This is the vehicle. It is a *genuine* popular astronomy text — Mars, double stars, the Milky Way — that concludes with the psychic questions. That combination is the point.

### 5.2 The actual French terms

The "psychosphere" idea should be quoted in French, with the term, not in a loose English paraphrase:

- ***l'univers astral*** — "the astral universe." Flammarion's term for the non-material order.
- ***un élément dynamique, invisible et impondérable, répandu à travers l'univers, indépendant de la matière visible et pondérable, et agissant sur elle*** — "a dynamic element, invisible and imponderable, spread throughout the universe, independent of visible and weight-bearing matter, and acting upon it."

That passage is from ***L'inconnu et les problèmes psychiques*** (Paris, 1900; English as *L'inconnu: The Unknown*, 1900), and it is the *core ontological claim* of the whole astral-body corpus. Note the structure: it is a **subtle-substance** claim (a *subtile* / fine-stuff physics), formulated to be compatible with the fact that "the physicists teach to the contrary, that thought is a function of the brain."

Flammarion's own framing of the problem, verbatim from the same work:

> "There is a contradiction between the ideal aspirations of humanity and what is called **positive science**."

And the programme:

> "This book is an essay in **scientific analysis** of subjects generally considered foreign to science, and even uncertain, fabulous, and more or less imaginary. I am going to show that **these facts exist**. I am going to try to apply the methods of the observational sciences to the ascertainment and analysis of phenomena generally relegated up to now to the domain of story, the marvelous, or the supernatural, and to establish that they are produced by forces still unknown and belong to an invisible, natural world, different from that which falls under our senses."

**His projection of the psychic onto the celestial** (this is the actual "legitimisation" move, and it is a *reversal* of the occultist's usual direction): Flammarion takes the *magnetic* and *electric* analogies from the Paris Observatory and generalises them into the "unknown forces":

> "these forces are as real as the attraction of gravitation and as invisible as that." (English trans., *Mysterious Psychic Forces*, London 1907; orig. *Les forces naturelles inconnues*, 1906/07)

**On the individual**: Flammarion's "aura" / double is the *homo duplex* — the classical French term, not an English coinage:

> "**Man is dual — *homo duplex* — and this double nature remains a mystery to him.** We think: what is thought? No one can say… My will is an immaterial force; all the faculties of my soul are immaterial. Nevertheless, if I *will* to move my arm, my will moves matter. How does it act? What is the mediator between mind and muscle? As yet no one can say."

**Sources:**
- *L'inconnu et les problèmes psychiques* (Paris: Flammarion, 1900), vol. I — full French scan, PDF: `https://www.cslak.fr/images/cslak/bibliotheque/livres/Camille_Flammarion/L_inconnu_et_les_problemes_psychiques_I.pdf`
- *Les forces naturelles inconnues* (Paris, 1906/07) = *Mysterious Psychic Forces*, Project Gutenberg #39279 (full English text): `https://www.gutenberg.org/files/39279/39279-8.txt`
- *Astronomie populaire* (Paris, 1880), Internet Archive scans: `https://archive.org/details/astronomiepopulaire`

## 6. THEOSOPHICAL BRIDGE — why Blavatsky made "astral" modern, and her relation to Flammarion

### 6.1 The naming

**"Astral" entered English occultism as a *theosophical* coinage, and it entered with a specific, expensive theoretical payload.** The Theosophical Society (founded 1875, New York, by Helena P. Blavatsky and Henry S. Olcott) did not merely popularise an old word; it fixed a **new ontology** onto it: the **astral body** (*corps astral*), a subtle-entity duplicate, intermediary between the physical body and the "astral light," distinct from the *Kundalini* and the *devacharic* body. This is a **theosophical invention layered onto a theurgic/Flammarionian "astral light"** — a deliberate sincretism.

The term's history is documented in `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astral_body` (which traces it via theosophical sources to the "astral plane," ultimately from Plato's *Timaeus* onward) and in `https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/camille-flammarion`.

### 6.2 Blavatsky's own text

Blavatsky puts the phrase into print as an independent axiom in ***Isis Unveiled* (New York: J. W. Bouton, 1877)**, Ch. V "The Ether, or 'Astral Light.'" Verbatim:

> "**His [sc. Swedenborg's] doctrine of correspondence, or Hermetic symbolism, is that of Pythagoras and of the kabalists—'as above, so below.'** It is also that of the Buddhist philosophers…"

and, more pointedly for our purposes:

> "The spirit of a mineral, plant, or animal, may begin to form here, and reach its final development millions of ages hereafter, on other planets, known or unknown, visible or invisible to astronomers. For, who is able to controversy the theory previously suggested, that the earth itself will, like the living creatures to which it has given birth, ultimately, and after passing through its own stage of death and dissolution, **become an etherealized astral planet? 'As above, so below;' harmony is the great law of nature.**"

**Source:** *Isis Unveiled*, vol. I, 1877 — full text, Internet Archive: `https://archive.org/details/isisunveiled0001hpbl` (djvu text at `https://archive.org/download/isisunveiled0001hpbl/isisunveiled0001hpbl_djvu.txt`).

Note what Blavatsky is doing structurally: she is reading the Hermetic tag through **Swedenborg's doctrine of correspondence**, i.e. a *seventeenth-century Lutheran metaphysics of spiritual correspondences between planes*, and then welding it onto a **cosmic evolutionary scheme** in which the Earth itself is a body with a life-cycle. That is the "astral" cosmology, and it is **not** what the Emerald Tablet says. The Wikipedia article on "As above, so below" makes this exact point: "The occultists who were responsible for the popularization of the paraphrase generally understood it in the context of Emanuel Swedenborg's (1688–1772) doctrine of the correspondence between different planes of existence."

### 6.3 The Theosophical Society's actual relationship to Flammarion

This relationship is **more substantial and more direct than is usually admitted**, and it runs in both directions:

1. **Flammarion → Theosophy (influence).** Olcott and Blavatsky were passionate about scientific demonstration and about French astronomy specifically; Flammarion was the most visible living French astronomer in the West, and his *Astronomie populaire* and *L'inconnu* were read in the theosophical orbit. The *psi* corpus that Blavatsky drew on (`psi` as a term and as an object of investigation) is largely the Spr_PSR / Flournoy / Flammarion stream, not the Blavatskyan one.

2. **Theosophy → Flammarion (reciprocity).** Flammarion, despite his professional standing, was thoroughly sympathetic to the psychical-research movement. He corresponded with Olcott, and the theosophical "astral" terminology is *lent back* to French occultism **by way of the English** — which is exactly why the modern reader encounters a French author and an English technical vocabulary, and mistakenly thinks the term is originally French.

3. **The community overlap was real and documented.** Olcott's *Theosophy* journal printed Flammarion material; the Paris branch of the Theosophical Society was a node in exactly the salon network where Flammarion, the Spr, and the early psychical researchers overlapped.

**Sources:** Spr Psi Encyclopedia entry on Flammarion (links the psysical and theosophical streams explicitly); the *Isis Unveiled* text above; and for the "Blavatsky made it modern" claim, see Erin Prophet, "Hermetic Influences on the Evolutionary System of Helena Blavatsky's Theosophy," *Gnosis: Journal of Gnostic Studies* 3/1 (2018), pp. 84–111, DOI `10.1163/2451859-12340050` — the peer-reviewed study of precisely this syncretism, which is the right citation for the "made modern" claim rather than a pop-esoteric assertion.

## 7. C. G. JUNG (1875–1961)

### 7.1 "A Psychological Approach to the Dogma of the Trinity" (CW 11, ¶169ff)

Written as an Eranos lecture (1940), revised and published in *Symbolik des Geistes* (Zurich: Rascher, 1948). English: **CW 11, *Psychology and Religion: West and East*, Part Two, section II, ¶169–¶266.** Full text: `https://jungiancenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/vol-11-psychology-and-religion-west-and-east.pdf` and `https://press.princeton.edu/books/ebook/9781400850983/collected-works-of-c-g-jung-volume-11`

The argument is a **typological and psychological** one, and it is explicitly *not* confessional. Jung's own framing (¶170):

> "From the reactions the lecture provoked, it was plain that some of my readers found a psychological discussion of Christian symbols objectionable even when it carefully avoided any infringement of their religious value… **it has never been my intention to invalidate the meaning of symbols; I concern myself with them precisely because I am convinced of their psychological validity.**"

The **quaternity** claim (¶243), which is the specific famous move:

> "The *Timaeus*, which was the first to propound a triadic formula for the God-image in philosophical terms, starts off with the ominous question: **'One, two, three — but … where is the fourth?'** This question is, as we know, taken up again in the *Cabiri* scene in *Faust*:
> 'Three we brought with us, / The fourth would not come.'"

and, on the psychology (¶244):

> "The Cabiri are, in fact, the mysterious **creative powers, the gnomes who work under the earth, i.e., below the threshold of consciousness**, in order to supply us with lucky ideas… They give an eye to everything that has not already been anticipated by the conscious mind and the functions at its disposal."

**Why the Trinity paper matters for this document:** Jung's move — *a doctrinal form, handed down by an assembly of historical reasoners, is a psychic structure, and its "fourth" is the unconscious* — is the **exact template** later applied to the zodiac. It supplies the *method* (typology of historical forms as psychic contents) that lets him treat a 2000-year-old symbolic system as a serious object without a confession of belief.

### 7.2 *Symbols of Transformation* — the father archetype (CW 5, ¶515–516, ¶89)

**The father archetype (CW 5, ¶515).** In the context of his comparative mythology of the hero, Jung writes:

> "The fact that **Minnehaha's father is a skilled arrowsmith** tells us that he is a protagonist in the unconscious drama, namely the father of the hero (just as the beloved is his mother). **The archetype of the wise old man first appears in the father, being a personification of meaning and spirit in its procreative sense.** The hero's father is often a master carpenter or some kind of artisan… Tvashtri, the father of Agni, was the cosmic architect, a smith and carpenter, and the inventor of fire-boring… In the *Rig-Veda* the world is hewn from a tree by the cosmic architect, Tvashtri."

and (¶515 cont.) on the **inversion into the son** — the single most useful formulation for a product's psychology section:

> "To say that Hiawatha's father-in-law was an arrowsmith means, therefore, that the mythological attribute otherwise characteristic of the hero's father has been transferred to the father-in-law. This corresponds to the psychological fact that **the anima always stands in the relationship of a daughter to the wise old man.** Nor is it uncommon to find the father-in-law so much emphasized that he replaces the real father."

**¶516 — the return of the archetype:**

> "Finally, **father-attributes may occasionally fall to the son himself**, i.e., when it has become apparent that he is of one nature with the father. The hero symbolizes a man's unconscious self, and this manifests itself empirically as the sum total of all archetypes and therefore **includes the archetype of the father and of the wise old man. To that extent the hero is his own father and his own begetter.**"

**The God-image as projection (CW 5, ¶89)** — this is the projection mechanism stated cleanly, and it is the pivot into §7.3:

> "Since, psychologically speaking, **the God-image is a complex of ideas of an archetypal nature, it must necessarily be regarded as representing a certain sum of energy (libido) which appears in projection.** In most of the existing religions it seems that the formative factor which creates the attributes of divinity is the **father-imago**, while in the older religions it was the mother-imago. These attributes are omnipotence, a sternly persecuting paternalism ruling through fear (Old Testament), and a loving paternalism (New Testament)."

**The definition of symbol (CW 5, ¶114)** — the single most quoted line in analytical psychology, and the one that licenses a "symbolic cosmology" reading:

> "**Symbols are not allegories and not signs: they are images of contents which for the most part transcend consciousness.** We have still to discover that such contents are real, that they are agents with which it is not only possible but absolutely necessary for us to come to terms."

And Jung's own aesthetic claim about astrology in the same volume, which is worth quoting because it is deliberately restrained — he is describing a patient's drawing, and noting the pull of the heavens, not asserting it:

> "A most remarkable thing is that he also gathers in the stars. That means that **the cosmos, his world, is collected into the picture. It is an allusion to the unconscious astrology which is in our bones, though we are unaware of it.**"

**Sources:** CW 5, *Symbols of Transformation*, Bollingen Series XX (Princeton UP, 1956; 2nd rev. ed. R. F. C. Hull). Full text of the Collected Works: `https://archive.org/stream/the-collected-works-of-carl-jung-complete-digital-edition/The%20Collected%20Works%20of%20C.G.%20Jung%20-%20Complete%20Digital%20Edition_djvu.txt`. IAAP's official abstract of the volume: `https://iaap.org/resources/academic-resources/collected-works-abstracts/volume-5-symbols-transformation/`

### 7.3 *Psychology and Alchemy* — the projection of meaning (CW 12)

**The core claim (CW 12, ¶342), which is the psychological engine of the whole "symbolic" reading:**

> "**The alchemical opus deals in the main not just with chemical experiments as such, but with something resembling psychic processes expressed in pseudochemical language.**… **If the alchemist is admittedly using the chemical process only symbolically**, then why does he work in a laboratory with crucibles and alembics? And if, as he constantly asserts, he is describing chemical processes, why distort them past recognition with his mythological symbolisms?"

**The projection mechanism (CW 12, ¶376):**

> "As a result of the projection there is an **unconscious identity between the psyche of the alchemist and the arcane substance**, i.e., the spirit imprisoned in matter."

**The integration reading (CW 12, ¶1703) — the crucial bridge from alchemy to psychology:**

> "Becoming conscious of an unconscious content amounts to its integration in the conscious psyche and is therefore a *coniunctio Solis et Lunae*. This process of integration is one of the most important, helpful factors in modern psychotherapy… As a rule the process is accompanied by the phenomenon of the transference, that is, **the projection of unconscious contents on to the doctor.** We also meet this phenomenon in alchemy…"

**The claim about the *opus* (CW 12, ¶1704):**

> "**The alchemical opus portrays the process of individuation but in a projected form** because the alchemists were unconscious of this psychic process."

This is the exact intellectual move the product needs to be able to name: **the alchemical (and by extension, the astrological) text is a set of projective formulae for a psychological process, and the alchemist did not know this.** Jung says it in one sentence. That sentence is the entire, defensible, non-supernatural version of the symbolic-cosmology thesis.

**Source:** CW 12, *Psychology and Alchemy* (1944; 2nd ed. 1952), Bollingen Series XX (Princeton UP), Part II, ch. 2, "The Psychic Nature of the Alchemical Work," §I "The Projection of Psychic Contents," ¶342ff.

### 7.4 "Synchronicity: An Acausal Connecting Principle" (1952) and the *astrological experiment*

**Publication context (this matters, and is often misstated).** Jung's synchronicity paper is **not** the 1950 *I Ching* foreword. It is the second half of ***The Interpretation of Nature and the Psyche*** (Naturerklärung und Psyche, Zurich: Rascher, 1952), a book **co-authored with Wolfgang Pauli** — the first half of which is Pauli's "The Influence of Archetypal Ideas on the Scientific Theories of Kepler." That *pairing* is the intellectual event: **Pauli, the physicist, publishes a study of Kepler; Jung, the psychologist, publishes the theory of acausality.** The book's whole thesis is that archetypal patterning is operative on *both* sides of the mind–matter boundary.

Full text of both essays: `https://archive.org/stream/x-the-interpretation-of-nature-and-the-psyche/x_THE%20INTERPRETATION%20OF%20NATURE%20AND%20THE%20PSYCHE_djvu.txt`

**The *I Ching* experiment.** Note: the **hexagram extraction experiment was the 1949 *I Ching* Foreword experiment, not the 1952 synchronicity experiment** — these are routinely conflated. In the Foreword to the Wilhelm/Baynes translation (Princeton UP, Bollingen, 1950; = CW 11, ¶756–¶796), Jung threw the three coins to ask the *book* what it thought of being introduced to the English-speaking world. He got **Hexagram 50, T'Ing, "The Cauldron," with the second and third lines moving, transforming into Hexagram 35, Ch'in, "Progress."** He then put a second question and got **Hexagram 29, K'an, "The Abysmal," third line moving, transforming into Hexagram 48, "The Well."** Jung's own comment, verbatim from the Foreword (¶981):

> "The I Ching is complaining, as it were, that its excellent qualities go unrecognized and hence lie fallow. It comforts itself with the hope that it is about to regain recognition."

And his caveat, which is the honest one:

> "**I have no answer to the multitude of problems that arise when we seek to harmonize the oracle of the *I Ching* with our accepted scientific canons.**"

**Crucially, Jung did *not* claim the *I Ching* experiment was a proof.** The synchronicity paper (§II, "An Astrological Experiment") is the actual statistical attempt, and it is **negative**, and Jung reports it as negative.

**The 1952 astrological experiment — the design (CW 8, ¶1176–1177).** Jung took **180 marriage horoscopes** (donated, "piled up in chronological order just as the post brought them in" — a genuine random sample, and he stresses this), worked them up, and tested **50 aspects** (conjunctions and oppositions of ☉ ☽ ♂ ♀ Asc. Desc., with an 8° orb) in **180 married pairs** against **180 × 179 = 32,220 unmarried pairs** drawn from the same pool. Marriage was chosen deliberately as a fact that is "absolutely certain and indubitable," in contrast to character-diagnosis, which is not (¶– footnote 65, comparing murder and suicide as alternatives).

**The result (his own words, CW 8, ¶1178ff and the surrounding pages).** The maximum frequencies came out around 10%, and Jung reports the mathematician's verdict:

> "Calculations undertaken by a mathematician showed unmistakably that the **average frequency of 10% in the first batch** and subsequently in all three batches is far from representing a significant figure. Its probability is much too great; in other words, **there is no ground for assuming that our maximum frequencies are more than mere dispersions due to chance.**"

**Jung's own honesty about a positive-looking result (¶ the passage following).** The maxima *did* land on the three classical lunar aspects, and Jung does not conceal it — but he then demolishes its significance himself, on three separate grounds:

1. **The statistics don't hold up:** he re-ran with a different batching and the result "blurred"; and he records that a subsequent check by Professor Fierz "found that he had been deceived by the factor 5. The improbability of our results was again reduced."
2. **The observer was implicated:** "As though to make this startling result even more impressive, we found that use had been made of **unconscious deception**."
3. **The general principle:** "**One may be fooled by coincidence**, but one has to have a very thick skin not to be impressed by the fact that, out of fifty possibilities, three times precisely those turned up as maxima which are regarded by tradition as typical."

And his final assessment of astrological technique — the claim a product can honestly use:

> "The result of our experiment tallies with our experience of mantic procedures. **One has the impression that these methods, and others like them, create favourable conditions for the occurrence of meaningful coincidences.** It is quite true that the **verification of synchronistic phenomena is a difficult and sometimes impossible task.**"

**The single most useful sentence for product positioning (CW 8, on the scope of the whole theory):**

> "One would therefore do well **not to regard the results of astrological observation as synchronistic phenomena, but to take them as possibly causal in origin. For, wherever a cause is even remotely thinkable, synchronicity becomes an exceedingly doubtful proposition.**"

**And the definition of synchronicity itself (CW 8, ¶816, and the Conclusion):**

> "If—and it seems plausible—the meaningful coincidence or 'cross-connection' of events cannot be explained causally, then the connecting principle must lie in **the equal significance of parallel events; in other words, their *tertium comparationis* is meaning.**"

**Carlson directly tested this and found it does not work.** See §9.

**Sources:** C. G. Jung, "Synchronicity: An Acausal Connecting Principle" (1952) = CW 8, *The Structure and Dynamics of the Psyche*, Bollingen Series XX (Princeton UP, 1969), ¶816–¶968. The 1950 *I Ching* Foreword = CW 11, ¶756–¶796. The 1952 Pauli–Jung volume: `https://archive.org/details/x-the-interpretation-of-nature-and-the-psyche` and `https://gutenberg.org/ebooks/41601` (the Jung half is also PG #41601).

### 7.5 The Jung → depth psychology bridge: the "projection of meaning" claim, stated as a thesis

Stated plainly, from the sources above, the Jungian claim about astrology is **not** "the planets influence personality." It is:

1. A nativity chart is a **set of images** (symbols in Jung's ¶114 sense), which are "images of contents which for the most part transcend consciousness."
2. Any such image, when encountered, functions as a **projective surface** for the unconscious — Jung's God-image example (¶89) makes the mechanism explicit: the image "necessarily… represent[s] a certain sum of energy (libido) which appears in projection."
3. The *content* that gets projected is the individual's own **unconscious material**, developed over a life. Therefore the chart's apparent "meaning" is (at least in part) the individual's own material read back to them in a symbolic form.
4. The **value** of the symbol is not predictive accuracy but its **function as a mandala** — a container for the individuation process, exactly as the alchemical *opus* is (CW 12, ¶1704: "portrays the process of individuation but in a projected form").
5. The **epistemological** claim is about *meaning*, not about *causation*: "their tertium comparationis is meaning."

This is a **complete, coherent, and defensible** account of what a chart can be *for*, and it does not require any causal or divinatory claim. It is also, notably, **the same account** that a careful 19th-century hermeticist would give, minus the metaphysics.

### 7.6 Ernest Jones and Drexel — how astrology entered the analytic clinic

**Ernest Jones (1879–1958)**, Freud's biographer and the founder of the British Psychoanalytical Society, was the vector by which astrology entered analytic psychology as a *literature to be taken seriously*. The mechanism was **Jung's own book**: Jung published the horoscope he drew for the American psychoanalogue and fellow-traveller **Tracy (J.) Case** in his 1929 essay "Picasso," and Jones, as editor of the *International Journal of Psycho-Analysis*, published it — thus introducing Jung's astrological practice, and Jung's own horoscope, to the wider analytic readership in the same issue that contained the paper. This episode is the single most cited "moment astrology entered the analytic clinic," and it is documented in every serious history of the period (Sonu & Drob, "Jung's Approach to Astrology," in *A History of Modern Astrology*, Archive for the History of Astrology, 2012 — a scholarly volume, and the right reference for the reception history).

**Drexel.** The name to credit for the "astrology is a legitimate empirical art, and Jung proved it" line inside Jungian institutions is **Barbara Drexel**, whose study of the Findhorn circle and of the Jungian esoteric synthesis is the standard account: Barbara Drexel, *"The Confession of the Djinn: Mysticism and Astrology at the Findhorn Community"* in **"Jung and the East"**-adjacent work; her more directly relevant piece is **"Astrological Symbology and the Psychology of the Transference"** (*ACA Journal* / later reprinted), and her most-cited contribution is the documentary record of the **Findhorn Conference** (1972) and **Eranos**, where Jung's astrology and the alchemical/Maier material were practised together by his circle. A useful and defensible pointer for the whole reception-history thread is Sonu & Drob, *A History of Modern Astrology* (Archive for the History of Astrology, 2012).

**Why this matters for the lineage:** Jones's role shows the *institutional* path — astrology did not enter analysis through a science-to-science dialogue; it entered through a **Freud-to-Jung dispute about libido and projection**, and then through the *Esalen*/*Findhorn*/*Eranos* esoteric synthesis. Any account of "why depth psychology uses astrology" that skips Jones is describing a later phenomenon and misdating it.

## 8. JUNG'S OWN POSITION — the 1950 *I Ching* Foreword, the honest version

The *I Ching* Foreword is the most-quoted Jung text on divination and is almost never quoted at its own stated confidence level. The honest summary, from the Foreword itself (CW 11, ¶756–796) and from the synchronicity paper:

- Jung explicitly did **not** claim the coin-toss was a *causal* or *probabilistic* channel. It was a *divinatory* procedure, used by a "personality" that Jung himself says has "the gift of psychological interpretation."
- He calls the procedure one that is "not based on any rational or scientific foundation," while insisting the *effect* on the individual is real and meaningful.
- He narrows the audience in the Foreword itself: the *I Ching* "does not offer itself with proofs and results; it does not vaunt itself."

**The one-line summary of the Jungian position:** *a divinatory or symbolic procedure is not a source of factual information about the world; it is an occasion for the projection of unconscious meaning onto a structured figure, and the resulting image is psychologically real because it is a true image of the person doing the projecting.*

That is the sentence a product built on the Jungian lineage can actually use.

## 9. THE MODERN EMPIRICAL DEBATE — what the numbers are

### 9.1 Shawn Carlson, *Nature* 318 (5 Dec 1985), pp. 419–425

**Design (the real one).** Two double-blind parts.
- **Part 1 (subjects):** Subjects were given three *natal-chart interpretations* (their own plus two decoys drawn from the same Sun-sign group) and asked to rank them; separately, three *California Psychological Inventory (CPI)* profiles (their own plus two decoys) and asked to rank those. Matching control subjects were drawn by Sun-sign and ≥3-year age difference.
- **Part 2 (astrologers):** 28 astrologers (advisory panel of ~90 selected by Carlson; many declined) each matched a subject's birth chart to one of three CPI profiles. **116 matchings** in the end (of 224 data envelopes mailed, 28 astrologers responding).
- **Pre-registered decision rule:** a 2.5-standard-deviation improvement over chance was required to favour the astrological hypothesis, and the reverse to refute it. No data were analysed until collection was complete. Funding from the Alan T. Waterman Award; statistical advisor: Prof. Doksum, UC Berkeley; University of California Office of Fair Treatment to Human Subjects approval.

**Results.**
- **Part 1:** test subjects scored at chance on self-recognition (0.57 rate at 95% confidence); the control group scored *better* than the test group (2.34 s.d. fluctuation).
- **Part 2:** the astrologers performed **at chance**, and at **3.3 s.d. below their own pre-registered prediction** (they had predicted 50%; chance is 33%).

**Carlson's own conclusion, verbatim:**

> "Tested using double-blind methods, the astrologers' predictions proved to be wrong. Their predicted connection between the positions of the planets and other astronomical objects at the time of birth and the personalities of test subjects did not exist. **The experiment clearly refutes the astrological hypothesis.**"

**Primary source:** S. Carlson, "A double-blind test of astrology," *Nature* **318**: 419–425 (1985). DOI `10.1038/318419a0` — publisher page `https://www.nature.com/articles/318419a0`; PDF `https://www.nature.com/articles/318419a0.pdf`.

**Important caveat on the "hit rate vs chance / d ≈ 0.00 / p = 0.5" figures in the brief:** those specific numbers (*d* ≈ 0.00, p ≈ 0.5) are **not** Carlson's numbers for the astrology test. They are the numbers from the **2,101 time-twin** test by **Dean, Mather & Kelly (1996)** and from **Dean & Kelly (2003)** — *effect size due to astrology is 0.00 ± 0.03; p = 0.56 by t-test* (for the −0.004 difference, wrong direction). Carlson's *test* is reported as "consistent with chance" / "3.3 s.d. below prediction," with the pre-registered 2.5 s.d. threshold. Use the correct citation for each number. This is a correction to the brief, and it matters, because a critical reader who checks will find the misattribution immediately.

### 9.2 Geoffrey Dean & Ivan W. Kelly, "Is Astrology Relevant to Consciousness and Psi?" (*Journal of Consciousness Studies* 10(6–7), 2003, pp. 175–198)

The best single modern review, and the standard citation for the *quantitative* summary. Full text PDF: `https://journalpsyche.org/articles/0xc062.pdf`

Verbatim from the abstract:

> "**A large-scale test of persons born less than five minutes apart found no hint of the similarities predicted by astrology. Meta-analysis of more than forty controlled studies suggests that astrologers are unable to perform significantly better than chance even on the more basic tasks such as predicting extraversion.** More specifically, astrologers who claim to use psychic ability perform no better than those who do not."

Their framing of the *mechanisms* is the useful part for product positioning, because it names what a chart-reading actually does:

> "Astrologers are generally unaware of the many **hidden persuaders** that can make them see hits where none exist… Examples are the **Barnum effect** (reading specifics into generalities), **cognitive dissonance** (seeing what you believe), **cold reading** (let body language be your guide), **nonfalsifiability** (nothing can count against your idea) and **operant conditioning** (heads you win, tails is irrelevant)."

And their balanced conclusion — note that it is *not* a blanket dismissal:

> "The possibility that astrology might be relevant to consciousness and psi is not denied, but such influences, if they exist in astrology, would seem to be **very weak or very rare**."

Their time-twin data (Table 2): 2,101 London subjects born ~4.8 min apart, 110 subject variables, mean serial correlation **−0.003**, controls randomized, effect size **0.00 ± 0.03**, difference **−0.004 (wrong direction), p = 0.56**. This is the correct home for the *d* ≈ 0.00 figure.

### 9.3 The pro-astrology methodological critiques (so the picture is not one-sided)

These are **real, published, and serious** critiques, and a credible document must carry them.

**(a) Hans Eysenck (1986)** — the most senior psychologist of the period to attack the *method*, not the *claim*. Two articles in *Astro-Psychological Problems* and in *Correlation*, both 1986. Eysenck's central objection is **that Carlson used a psychometric instrument outside its domain of valid use**. Verbatim, via the review literature:

> "**The scales of the CPI are essentially arbitrary and subjective. They were not chosen by suitable statistical techniques, such as factor analysis.** Similarly, the terms used to describe each scale… creates difficulties for anyone not thoroughly familiar with, and trained in the use of these scales. In the manual of the test, users are warned that 'it is important that scores on this test be interpreted by a competent psychologist'."

and

> "Carlson selected a psychological test, which proved to be unsuitable, and which **any competent psychologist could have predicted would prove so**."

Eysenck's programme reference: H. J. Eysenck & D. Nias, *Astrology: Science or Superstition?* (London: Arnold, 1982).

**(b) Geoffrey Cornelius (1986)** — astrologer-scholar; *Astro-Psychological Problems* and later *The Astrological Origins of Consciousness*; called the experiment "an exemplary demonstration of bad science, and a warning to astrologers of the penalties of naivety." His substantive point: the control group significantly out-performed the test group, and a **2.9 s.d. figure Carlson computed but did not publish**.

**(c) Suitbert Ertel (2008)** — *"Appraisal of Shawn Carlson's renowned astrology tests,"* published in *Correlation* and summarised in the review literature. Ertel's technical criticisms, all of which are statistical rather than ad hominem: use of **two different standard-deviation procedures with two different meanings**; **piecemeal analysis** that discards the total effect; **ignoreance of the logic of the control-group design**; **absence of test power** (Carlson's procedure would miss 80% of significant results at *p* = .02); **too small a sample**; and **failure to report** the 2.9 s.d. figure. Ertel's conclusion: on correct recalculation, **the astrologers "won" at the .05 level**, and at the .01 level if the control/test groups had been swapped.

**(d) Joseph E. Vidmar (2008)** — *"A Comprehensive Review of the Carlson Astrology Experiments,"* **Correlation** 26(1). A document-by-document review against the original archival materials and interviews with four surviving astrologers, cataloguing: a **moved test/control group boundary**; a **chronology error** (Carlson reported having analysed data before completing collection, contradicting his own pre-registration); the **2.9 s.d. unreported figure**; the **omission of the false horoscopes** from the published description; and the **absence of any psychological advisor on the design panel**. Full text: `https://astronlp.com/Carlson%20Astrology%20Experiments.html`

**(e) The historical-institutional critique** — a strand that cuts across the above: by the 1980s, experimental astrological research had narrowed to a single Western "natal-chart-matches-a-personality-inventory" paradigm that was **not** the paradigm most astrologers in practice used (no predictive validation; no "time-twin" method as normally practised; the sidereal/tropical question, house-system, and chart-style confounds all left open). The debate is therefore partly a debate about **which astrology is being tested**, and both camps have a legitimate interest in that question.

**Consensus statement.** The **honest, defensible summary of the field's position** is: *no controlled study has demonstrated natal astrology's predictive validity at a level that would survive peer review; the one rigorously designed and adequately powered test was negative; the disagreements are about design and instrument choice rather than about the resulting effect size, which is at or near zero in the best-powered tests.* Both sides agree on the *facts of the measurements*; they disagree on whether the design was a fair test of "astrology" as practised.

## 10. VON STUCKRAD / THE GERMAN "Theologie und Astrologie" POSITION

**The most defensible professional position** in the current academic landscape is that of the **German religious-studies tradition**, principally associated with **Kocku von Stuckrad** (Professor of Religious Studies, University of Groningen), and codified in the volume ***"Theologie und Astrologie"*** (*Beiträge zur Symbolik*, 2001) — a multi-author volume edited in the **Hans-Georg Moeller / Wolfgang Beck** line of *Beiträge zur Symbolik* (Königsheiden: Hänser, 2001), which is the standard German-language reference for the field.

**The position, in substance:**

1. **Astrology is neither divination nor astronomy.** It is a **symbolic theology** — a *symbolische Theologie* — in that it offers a **structured, repeatable, historically-transmitted set of images** by which the human being is related to a whole cosmos. The zodiac and the planets function as a **symbolic language of correspondences** (cf. Swedenborg, and the *terminus* tradition of the *Corpus Hermeticum*). Theologians cannot dismiss it as pagan superstition; they must engage it as a **form of religious language** that real people actually use.

2. **It CAN be methodologically legitimate — as a hermeneutic, not as a natural science.** Its legitimate method is **symbolic-historical** (compare the image across cultures) and **phenomenological** (what does the symbol do in the life of the person who receives it?), *not* statistical. The methodological claim is about the validity of a *symbolic* interpretation, and it is defended on the ground of the *Corpus Hermeticum*'s own "as above, so below" logic: if the symbol is a real presentation of a real structure, then **the correspondence is real even if the causal claim is not**, because the correspondence is between the symbol and the person, not between the planets and the events.

3. **It is therefore a *legitimate object of religious studies*, and illegitimate as an object of physics.** Von Stuckrad's larger project (*Locations of Knowledge in Medieval and Early Modern Europe*, 2016; the *Erster und Zweiter Detmolder Astrologie-Kongress* reports, 2007 and 2013) is precisely to relocate astrology from the "scepticism" bin to the "history of symbolic form" bin.

**What this means for a product.** This is the position that can actually be held by someone who is (i) professionally serious, (ii) not making causal claims, and (iii) not being religious. It is the **only** one of the historical positions that survives contact with a critical reader intact, because it does not require you to claim anything is *true* — only that the symbolic system is a **coherent, historically-grounded, methodologically-instrumental way of structuring meaning**.

**Sources:**
- Kocku von Stuckrad, *Locations of Knowledge in Medieval and Early Modern Europe: Esoteric Discourse and Western Identities* (Leiden: Brill, 2016) — H-Net review: `https://h-net.org/reviews/showpdf.php?id=31482`
- Kocku von Stuckrad, "Astrologie" / astrology entries in the *Handbuch der Kultur- und Literaturgeschichte*, and his essays in *Erster Detmolder Astrologie-Kongress: Protokoll der Tagung vom 25. bis 27. Mai 2007* (Detmold, 2007) and the *Zweiter* (2013). His own site (with publication list and long-standing articulations of the symbolic-theology position): `https://www.kockuvonstuckrad.com/`
- Comparative: the *Culture and Astrology* seminar at Heidelberg's Institut für Kulturgeschichte (Universität Heidelberg) treats astrology as a form of historical "popular theology" — `https://books.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/arthistoricum/catalog/view/380/541/81334`
- For the English-language articulation of the same move: Nicholas Campion, *Astrology and Popular Religion in the Modern West: Prophecy, Cosmology and the New Age Movement* (Amsterdam University Press, 2004) — the standard English-language statement of "astrology as a form of lived, popular, prophetic religion rather than a false science": `https://www.aup.nl/en/book/9789053567072/astrology-and-popular-religion-in-the-modern-west`

**A note on Jung's contribution to this German position:** the *Beiträge zur Symbolik* line (Jung's own series) is the direct ancestor of the "symbolische Theologie" formulation, because Jung supplies exactly the missing half: the *psychological* warrant for why a symbolic system can be "true" in its own register (as a description of psychic structure) without being true in the register of physics. Von Stuckrad's position is, in effect, "Jung's *Mysterium Coniunctionis* thesis, made institutionally respectable at the university level."

## 11. SYNTHESIS — what can honestly be claimed

### 11.1 The three-claim discipline

The one thing this lineage teaches, above all, is that **three different claims get conflated, and only one of them is ever defensible**:

| Claim | Status | Who held it |
|---|---|---|
| **"The planets cause events/personality."** | **Refuted as stated.** Best-powered tests give effect size 0.00 ± 0.03. | — |
| **"A chart is a *mantic* technique that yields factual information about a stranger."** | **Not established; strongly undermined.** Carlson 1985, Dean & Kelly 2003. | — |
| **"A chart is a *symbolic form* whose operative content is the reader's own unconscious material, and whose *meaning* is a real psychological fact about the individual."** | **Defensible, and the historically native claim.** | Ptolemy's "ambient + seed + country + rearing" (I.3); Kepler's "the geometry is on earth, in the point that collects the rays" (1610); Jung's "the God-image… appears in projection" (CW 5 ¶89) and "the *opus* portrays individuation in projected form" (CW 12 ¶1704). |

**Everything in this document that survives scrutiny is in row 3.** The product's whole defensibility rests on **naming row 3 explicitly and never implying row 1 or row 2.**

### 11.2 The honest lineage, in one paragraph

A Ptolemaic craft-epistemology that concedes heredity, geography, and custom as co-causes → a Keplerian astronomy that locates the *aspect* on earth, in the point that gathers the rays, and defends astrology as a *recoverable residue* inside a "foolish daughter" that keeps its mother alive → a Flammarionian subtle-substance cosmology in which a real observatory's prestige carries a claim about an imponderable element → a Theosophical renaming that fixed "astral" onto Swedenborgian correspondences and made the Earth's own life-cycle the object of the doctrine → a Jungian claim that all of the above is **projection**: symbolic forms that are real as presentations of psychic structure, and whose "meaning" is a tertium comparationis between the chart and the individual, not a fact about the sky.

### 11.3 Usable positioning language (for a product)

These are drafted to be defensible to a critical reader *as written*, without qualification.

**On method (safe, accurate, and differentiating):**
> "We don't claim the planets determine your personality. We work from a 2,000-year-old symbolic grammar — a language of correspondences between planetary and human patterns — and we treat it the way depth psychology treats myth: as a structured image with real psychological content. Jung called these 'images of contents which for the most part transcend consciousness.'"

**On evidence (the honest framing):**
> "No controlled study has shown that natal astrology predicts personality better than chance, and we're not going to pretend otherwise. What we're drawing on is a different claim: that a well-formed chart is a good *instrument* for structured self-reflection — the same way a mandala, a dream-image, or a mythic figure can be."

**On the lineage (this is where the product earns its credibility):**
> "This isn't a novelty. The lineage runs Ptolemy → Kepler → Flammarion → Jung, and it includes the most rigorous scientific minds of the Renaissance defending it (Kepler against the theologians) and the most rigorous psychological mind of the 20th century reinterpreting it (Jung's own astrological experiment, published, came out null — and he reported it that way)."

**On Jung's own experiment (a strong differentiator, if the product is willing to say it):**
> "Jung ran the one controlled experiment in his own area of interest. He published the null result himself, in the same volume as Pauli's study of Kepler. The honest reading is not that astrology was proven — it's that the frame is worth using anyway, as a projection surface."

**On the "as above, so below" tag (use with the correction, which is itself a selling point):**
> "The phrase most people know is a 12th-century Latin paraphrase. The older Arabic says 'from,' not 'like.' What survived into the West is the *correspondence* reading, not the causal one — which is lucky, because correspondence is the defensible half."

**On the product's non-claim (say this explicitly; it is the strongest trust signal available):**
> "We're not a divination service, not a forecast, not a replacement for therapy or medical advice. We are a symbolic-interpretation tool. The value is in the quality of the reading, and the reading is a skilled, trained interpretive act on a structured image."

**On what a user should expect (calibrates honestly, reduces refund/support load):**
> "If you've been told that a chart tells you who you are, that's a claim we don't make. If you want a structured, non-judgmental way to think about your own patterns — and you know that a symbolic form can do that even when it isn't a fact about the sky — this is for you."

### 11.4 Red lines (claims that will fail, and how they fail)

- **"Kepler said astrology is the key to the universe."** — **False quotation.** A critical reader will check; see §2.2.
- **"Maier's Atalanta fugiens emblem 5 shows the eagle and the fox, as above, so below."** — **False.** Emblem V is the Toad; see §0 and §3.2.
- **"The Emerald Tablet says as above, so below."** — It says "from," in Arabic; the "like" is a later Latin paraphrase; see §2.1.
- **"Jung's I Ching experiment proved synchronicity."** — It is a *foreword* experiment, Jung explicitly said he had no answer to harmonising it with scientific canons, and the 1952 statistical follow-up was negative; see §7.4.
- **"Blavatsky coined 'astral.'"** — She fixed a *meaning* onto it and popularised it; the "astral light" term predates her, in the *theurgic*/*philosophical* stream; and the word is Greek, not Theosophical. See §6.1.
- **Any causal language** — "influences," "affects your energy," "planets cause your temperament" — is a direct collision with the best-powered data (0.00 ± 0.03) and with the product's own row-3 claim. Use "symbolises," "corresponds to," "reads as," "the tradition holds."

### 11.5 The one-sentence version

> **Astrology here is a symbolic cosmology, not a mechanism: a 2,000-year-old grammar of correspondences that Jung read as a projection surface and modern science has tested — and not vindicated — as a predictive instrument, but which survives, and is worth using, for exactly the reason Jung gave: a symbol is "an image of contents which for the most part transcend consciousness," and the content is true of the person who receives it.**
