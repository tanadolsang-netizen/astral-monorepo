## What the article covers

A systematic reference guide to the main predictive (event-timing) methods used in classical Vedic/Jyotish astrology — going beyond the Panchang/Sade Sati/Dasha basics already captured in `raw/2026-08-13-gurudev-astrology-free-will.md` into the fuller technical toolkit practitioners layer together.

## The nine techniques covered

1. **Lagna (Ascendant)** — the foundational reference point that determines the entire house structure of the chart. The article stresses this is where Vedic practice diverges sharply from popular Western sun-sign culture: Lagna, not the Sun, is "the most important reference point in Vedic astrology" for prediction.

2. **The 12 Houses (Bhavas)** — each governs a fixed life domain (wealth, relationships, career, health, etc.), with consistent significations carried across every divisional chart, not just the main birth chart.

3. **Navamsha (D9) chart** — a divisional chart that subdivides each 30° sign into nine segments of 3°20' each. Used specifically to check planetary "maturity" and marriage/partnership quality. Key line from the source: **"A planet that is strong in D1 but weak in D9 will fail to deliver its full promise"** — i.e., the main birth chart (D1) shows potential, but the D9 shows whether that potential actually pays off.

4. **Vimshottari Dasha system** — the same 120-year, nine-planet cycle referenced in the existing Gurudev source, but with more structural detail here: each planet's Mahadasha (major period) is subdivided further into **Antardasha** and **Pratyantar Dasha** (sub-periods within sub-periods) to narrow event timing down to specific windows rather than multi-year blocks.

5. **Transits (Gochar)** — ongoing planetary movement, especially of **Saturn and Jupiter**, is described as the trigger mechanism that activates whatever a person's current Dasha period has already "promised" — the dasha sets the theme, the transit fires the event.

6. **Retrograde planets** — retrograde motion is treated as intensifying a planet's effects and internalizing/delaying them. The article notes an asymmetry: a **benefic** planet retrograde can produce exceptionally good results, while a **malefic** planet retrograde intensifies difficulty — i.e., retrograde amplifies whatever the planet's basic nature already was, rather than uniformly helping or hurting.

7. **Ashtakavarga** — a point-scoring system that assigns numeric strength ("bindus") to each house across all planets combined. Concrete thresholds given: scores **above 28** indicate strong results for that house/period; scores **below 25** suggest challenges.

8. **Parashari and Jaimini systems** — two parallel classical frameworks used to cross-check each other. Parashari (the more commonly taught system) is planetary-period-based; Jaimini is sign-based and introduces distinct concepts like **Chara Karakas** (variable planetary significators assigned by degree within a chart rather than fixed by house) for independent verification of the same predicted events.

9. **Nirayan (sidereal) zodiac** — explicitly named as the astronomical foundation of all the above, using actual star positions rather than the tropical/seasonal zodiac, with the same ~23–24° ayanamsha gap described in the sidereal-vs-tropical source above.

## Integration framework (how practitioners combine these)

The article's closing framing: **Dasha identifies what's karmically promised → transits (Gochar) trigger when it activates → Ashtakavarga measures how strong the result will be → Navamsha confirms whether the promise actually delivers → Jaimini cross-validates the timing** via an independent method. This is presented as the standard operating stack, not an either/or menu.
